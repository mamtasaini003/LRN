# Numerical Results and Discussion

In this section, we present a comprehensive evaluation of the Latent Reciprocity Neural Fourier Neural Operator (LRR-FNO) across five distinct experimental regimes, following the benchmarks established in Section 3. We compare our method against a standard Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) baseline, demonstrating the impact of latent-space alignment on accuracy, robustness, and representation learning.

## 1. Physical Field Prediction Accuracy (Experiment 1)
We first evaluate the predictive performance on steady-state and transient physical regimes. As shown in Table \ref{tab:steady_state}, LRR-FNO consistently outperforms vanilla FNO across various geometries and forcing functions. Notably, for the Poisson equation on a circular domain, LRR-FNO achieves a **+31.25\%** improvement in relative $L_2$ error. In more complex anisotropic domains, such as the Ellipse with an aspect ratio of 2.5, we observe improvements up to **+39.78\%**. Figure \ref{fig:field_comparison} (Ref: `results/exp_1/exp1_summary_comparison.png`) illustrates that LRR-FNO more accurately captures sharp gradient transitions and heterogeneous field distributions, which are typically blurred by the spectral bias of standard FNO.

## 2. Latent Space Alignment (Experiment 2)
A core contribution of the LRR framework is the alignment of the model's internal backbone features ($v_K$) with the physical manifold of the ground-truth solutions ($z_u$). We quantify this alignment using cosine similarity. As reported in Figure \ref{fig:latent_alignment} (Ref: `results/exp_2/Circle_latent_reps.png` and `darcy_latent_tsne.pdf`), LRR-FNO achieves a near-perfect similarity score of **0.9736** with the true solution latent space, compared to a disorganized representation in the baseline FNO ($Sim \approx 0.11$). This alignment proves that LRR-FNO internalizes the underlying physics of the dynamical system, organizing its representation space based on meaningful physical parameters rather than mere pixel-wise correlations.

## 3. Zero-shot Super-Resolution (Experiment 3)
We further investigate the resolution-invariant properties of the learned latent space. By training at a resolution of $32 \times 32$ and evaluating zero-shot at $64 \times 64$, we observe that LRR-FNO maintains higher fidelity in high-frequency regions compared to the baseline. Figure \ref{fig:super_res} (Ref: `results/exp_3/Circle_super_resolution.png`) demonstrates that the reciprocity constraint acts as a powerful regularizer that prevents the loss of detail when upscaling to finer discretizations.

## 4. Robustness to Observational Noise (Experiment 4)
Physical data is often corrupted by noise. We evaluate the resilience of the learned representations by injecting $5\%$ Gaussian noise into the input permeability fields. As shown in Figure \ref{fig:noise_robustness} (Ref: `results/exp_4/Circle_noise_robustness.png`), LRR-FNO exhibits significantly lower sensitivity to noise than the baseline FNO. The latent projection mechanism effectively filters high-frequency noise, mapping the corrupted input to the stable, physically-consistent manifold learned during the Latent Reciprocity stage.

## 5. Out-of-Distribution Generalization (Experiment 5)
Finally, we test the generalization capabilities of LRR-FNO on out-of-distribution (OOD) forcing fields and domain geometries. Models trained on simple circular forcing are evaluated on complex elliptic and multi-blob distributions. Figure \ref{fig:ood_gen} (Ref: `results/exp_5/Circle_ood_generalization.png`) highlights that LRR-FNO maintains robust performance under distribution shift. While the baseline FNO error degrades significantly on OOD geometries, the reciprocity inductive bias ensures that the model correctly maps novel inputs to the universal physical manifold of the PDE, rather than overfitting to specific training geometries.
